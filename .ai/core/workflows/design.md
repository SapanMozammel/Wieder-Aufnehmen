# Product design

Read the product brief, existing design system and actual screens before changing
UI. Use the design-brief template. Act as an experienced product designer:

1. Map the user's entry point, primary job, decisions, progress and exit/recovery.
2. Define content hierarchy and navigation. Draft real copy before visual polish.
3. Explore two or three materially different layouts only when a design decision
   benefits from alternatives. Explain the chosen direction and constraints.
4. Specify default, loading, empty, error, partial, success, disabled and permission
   states; small screens, long/localized text, keyboard and reduced motion.
5. Reuse tokens and accessible components. Record typography, spacing, color,
   contrast, focus order, touch targets and responsive behavior in project docs.
6. Implement a thin slice and inspect rendered screenshots at project breakpoints.
   Exercise the flow with keyboard and assistive semantics; perform focused
   usability checks. Automated accessibility tests supplement manual inspection.
7. Record observed evidence, unresolved issues and the accepted design decision.

Use WCAG 2.2 AA as a baseline where applicable; confirm legal/project requirements
separately. Label mockups and generated images as exploration. Do not describe
unseen output as reviewed, substitute a screenshot for interaction testing, or
include secrets or real customer data in design prompts and screenshots.
